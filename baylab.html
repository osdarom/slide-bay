Rules v2 · what this model assumes

A car sits on its own platform, three cells long and one wide; a cell is one stall width, 6′10″. A platform slides any distance along its own length if the path is clear, or rotates 90° anywhere if the 3 × 3 block around its middle is clear (it sweeps the corner cells as it turns). Platforms that are not the car being retrieved can link into a train and slide together. One step is one moment of simultaneous motion: any number of platforms each make one slide or one rotation, starting together at the same speed; a step is legal only if no two platforms ever overlap, checked one cell of travel at a time. A car sweeping into space another is leaving in the same step is allowed when the timing works.

The floor is the Wells example plate, 243′4″ × 122′, two 61′ column-free bays with one interior column line every 36′. Lifts are fixed slots (baseline: one at each end, in line with the exit lane); a retrieval ends when the car lies on a lift, and the lift's cycle is added to its time.

Storage rules (v1–v2). No car is stored where it can never turn (lengthwise against a wall: its 3 × 3 would run off the floor, so it could never reach a lift). Every column keeps one free slot, and the free slots line up into an exit lane next to the lifts' row. With uneven demand, the busy fleet cars are stored next to the lane. The promise: 95% of retrievals reach a lift within 15 steps (the 15 is assumed until real speeds are sourced).

The planner finds the car's cheapest route to a lift and clears every platform in the way (with trains and short chains of clearing moves); it tries several routes, keeps up to four plans that work, and uses the one with the fewest steps. If none works, a search lets the car and the platforms around it take turns (as dense robot fleets do); a step-by-step re-planner is the last resort. Each move goes into the earliest step it can safely join, so platforms with room clear the way in anticipation instead of waiting for the car. It is a heuristic: on small bays the exhaustive search checks it. Not modelled yet: arrivals, charging, floor slope (real decks slope 1–1.5%), more than one floor, and several retrievals at once.
