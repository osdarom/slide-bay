What this model assumes

A car is inert cargo on a pallet, three cells long and one wide. The car drawn on each pallet is a typical sedan, about 16′ × 6′, at the scale of a 6′10″ stall; the puzzle only sees the pallet.The building slides it along its own axis into any free cell — that is a shuttle running under the pallet, and it costs one move. A car cannot move sideways at all — a vertical pallet never changes column until it is turned. To change axis its centre must stand on a cross-aisle, a row the building has equipped to rotate a pallet ninety degrees, with the swept footprint clear. The exit row is always a cross-aisle; every other one is a row of storage given up to machinery. The west opening accepts a car only when it lies east–west on the exit row. Nothing here models acceleration, pallet exchange time, queueing at the opening, or what happens when two retrievals are wanted at once.

The controller is a best-first search with a fixed budget of bay states it is allowed to open. When it runs out, the bay says OVER BUDGET, not impossible — the way out may exist and simply not have been found. That distinction is the honest one: a real controller also has a deadline, and a retrieval it cannot plan in time is a retrieval that does not happen. The heuristic here is crude, so these budgets are the cost of this controller, not a lower bound on any controller. A3 would have to measure a real one.
