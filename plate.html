Every assumption in this layer
  • kVA is not kW. Capacity is kVA × power factor = kW real. Chargers are assumed near-unity PF.
  • Rated output is not draw. Level 2 at kW / efficiency; DC fast at kW / . The service sees the larger number.
  • No diversity factor is assumed. Worst case is every point at full rate. Actual draw falls out of the fleet's own charge state instead of being discounted by a rule of thumb.
  • DC tapers above % charge to of rated. Level 2 does not taper.
  • Battery is kWh for every car. Real fleets are mixed.
  • Fire separation is a spacing rule, not a standard. Charge points keep cells clear of each other (Chebyshev). NFPA 88A and FM guidance are more complex than one number.
  • The cap is hard. Over budget, the lowest-charge cars are served first and the rest simply do not charge. A real depot would use load management.
  • Charging only advances while retrievals run. There is no idle overnight window in this model — which is exactly when a real garage would do most of its charging.
Physical parametersall tweakable
8′6″ is the human stall, sized by the door swing. 6′10″ is the A1 re-striped bay. Below the swing threshold a car can no longer turn without a free cell beside it.
Moves allowed before the retrieval is refused as too slow. One move = one car sliding one bay.
A wall with a single opening lets a car block the only door it needs. A sealed bay cuts the far field off from the exit entirely.
A robotaxi dispatcher does not want your car, it wants a car. Switching this is the difference between a garage that stores property and one that stores stock.
The toy cannot know this. It is an operations decision, and it changes the fleet result more than any physical law in the model does.

Retrieval follows the escort method for puzzle-based storage systems (Gue & Kim, 2007): an empty cell is walked to the bay ahead of the requested car and swapped, repeatedly, until the car reaches the exit. Refusal is computed, not scored — a locked plate is one where no empty can reach that bay.
